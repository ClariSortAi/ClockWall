## What is done and verified

Not "looks right". Each of these is a measurement with an answer.

The tooth counts are counted off the solids, not chosen: escape wheel 20, escape
pinion 8, fourth wheel 84, so the train ratio is 10.5. The previous 15, 7 and 64
were invented, and they set the beat and every rotation rate under it.

The beat follows from those counts rather than sitting beside them. The fourth
wheel carries the seconds so it must turn once a minute, which puts the caliber
at 25,200 vph. At the old 28,800 that wheel went round in 52.5 seconds and would
have drifted visibly against the seconds hand. The dial reads the rate from the
same derivation now instead of having it typed in, so it says 3.5 Hz, 25,200 vph.

The whole escapement is placed by one similarity transform, so every ratio and
angle is inherited from a movement that works rather than re-derived. Three
independent checks agree that the placement is right:

- The cock's balance jewel hole, read out of the cock's own outline, lands
  0.0046 mm from the balance arbor read separately off the assembly.
- Both pallet stones reach inside the escape wheel's tooth-tip circle by 0.160
  and 0.158 mm. Equal lock on entry and exit is what a correctly set escapement
  has.
- The stones sit 62.08 degrees apart, which on a 20-tooth wheel is 6.90 half
  tooth-spaces, so 7. The file's own reasoning says the count must be odd or the
  wheel cannot alternate. It is odd. Our figure had been 5.

`scripts/inspect validate` passes with zero failures. `tools/interfere_check.py`
reports seven contacts, all of them press fits and bearings that are declared
with a reason, and no part passing through another.

The app builds clean and runs. The escape wheel, the fourth wheel, the lever with
its ruby stones, the balance and the hairspring are all genuinely real geometry
and look it at close crop.

## What I got wrong

**I built before I looked for what existed.** The first instinct was to model a
balance wheel in CadQuery from scratch. A real, licence-clean Swiss movement was
one search away, and it took two prompts from the user to get me there. The right
order is: does this exist, can I use it, what is the smallest thing I write. I
ran it backwards.

**I did not check the skill list against my own plan.** The `cad` skill's
description is almost a word-for-word match for "inspect a STEP assembly, extract
solids, place them, export STL, take measurements". I wrote three files of raw
OCP first and burned iterations on an assembly-traversal bug and a tooth-count
sampling bug, both of which that skill's CLI already handles. The habit that
fixes this is a five-second scan of the skill list keyed on the nouns in my own
plan, the moment the plan contains them.

**I reasoned from plausibility instead of measuring, repeatedly.** I called
`OM10-00104` the escape wheel twice before a plan view showed it is the pallet
fork. I said `OM10-00197` was "probably the ratchet wheel" with nothing behind
it. Every identification I got wrong came from a size-and-position argument, and
every one that held up came from a measurement: the angular-FFT tooth count, and
the observation that both pallet stones sit exactly 3.00 mm from the escape
centre. When I catch myself thinking "that is about the right size for X", that
is the signal to go find the number that decides it.

**I let a weak check stand in for a real one.** After placing the cock I tested
"nearest vertex to the balance axis is 2.98 units" and called the placement
confirmed. That test cannot fail: the cock's boss is near the balance under any
rotation. The real invariant, that the cock's jewel hole lands ON the arbor, was
off by 14 units and I did not find it for another hour, and then only by accident
while chasing a screw. A check that cannot fail is not a check.

**Three self-inflicted bugs, all invisible in output that looked fine.**
Re-running the extractor silently restored its own broken tooth counts and made
the train ratio 84 divided by zero. Taking the stack datum from the lowest solid
floated the entire movement 23 units off the plate, because pinion arbors
legitimately hang below the wheels. And the rotation sign bug below. Each is now
guarded or commented at the place it was created, which is what I should have
done when I created it rather than after it bit.

**The rotation bug is the one worth studying.** `om10_layout.to_face` rotates
points in face coordinates, where y runs down. `place()` rotated solids in
Blender's frame, where y runs up. The same angle turns opposite ways in the two
frames, so every solid ended up spun 13.2 degrees away from the layout that
positioned it. It survived because it is nearly invisible: every wheel is
rotationally symmetric so spinning one changes nothing you can see, and every
part still landed at the right centre because centres come from `to_face`. Only
long parts showed it. Fixing it made five separate interference pairs disappear,
including the gear mesh, which is how I know it was real.

**I spent too long on identification and not enough on finish.** The geometry is
right and the picture still is not good. That is a misallocation, and it is the
reason the brief is not met.

## Why it still does not look ten out of ten

The geometry is real. The **finish and the framing** are not, and that is where
the remaining gap lives.

**The balance cock renders as a bright white slab.** It is the largest and
brightest object in the aperture and it flattens under a straight-down camera,
because its top face is broad, flat and pointed at the light. Real cocks are
bright, but what makes them read is perlage or côtes on top and a polished
anglage running round the edge, both of which catch light at an angle to the top
face. Our `steel` material gives a near-uniform top and a hard edge.

**The cock covers about a third of the balance, and it may be the wrong side of
the watch.** Here is a hypothesis I did not verify and the next agent should:
we are rendering the movement from the caseback side, where the balance cock sits
above the balance and hides it. An open-heart dial aperture conventionally looks
at the movement so the balance reads clearly. Flipping the z-order of the cock
relative to the balance, or dropping the cock from the aperture entirely, may be
both more authentic for this dial and much better looking. Treat that as
something to check against reference photographs, not as a fact from me.

**The plate is still ours and it shows.** A 6.5-unit disc with bores and a
generic graining, against real parts. It reads as horizontal banding. The OM10's
own mainplate is sitting in `captures/om10/parts/mainplate.step` and has real
countersinks and real bores. It is 363 face units across against a 252-unit
aperture, so using it is a framing decision, not a drop-in.

**The framing was inherited rather than reconsidered.** I kept the balance's size
and position from the old invented layout and scaled a real movement to fit it.
That is why the cock is enormous relative to the window: we are seeing a 21 mm
slice of a 31 mm movement. Shrinking the movement so more of it fits would make
every part smaller but the composition more like a real openworked watch, where
you see several bridges and a lot of plate rather than three big wheels.

## What I was missing

**A fast shaded preview, and this is the big one.** Every visual question in this
session cost eight minutes because `render_lib` is hardwired to Cycles at 256
samples and renders eleven passes at 1920px. An EEVEE preview of one crop at low
samples would answer "is the cock still a white slab" in twenty seconds. I built
`tools/layout_preview.py` for the geometry question, which helped a lot and took
ten minutes to write, and I should have built the shading equivalent too. Whoever
picks this up should build it before touching a material.

**A reference-image workflow.** There is no skill or connector here for pulling
reference photographs and putting them next to a render for comparison. For a
brief whose success criterion is "looks real", working without a target is the
central handicap, and it is why the whole session had no way to tell "better"
from "good".

**Something that asserts geometric invariants after placement.** The rotation bug
would have died in seconds against a test that says "the cock's jewel hole is
within a unit of the balance arbor, the lever's fork points at the roller, the
screws sit in their holes". Those assertions are three lines each and I wrote
them only as one-off diagnostics while chasing a symptom. They belong in a file
that runs after every placement change.

**A material-authoring path at this fidelity.** `blender-toolkit` does not fit:
it drives a live GUI over its own addon socket and its material vocabulary is
create/set-colour/set-metallic, which cannot express the node graphs
`render_lib` already has, and our render is headless. I looked and said so rather
than forcing it. What is actually needed is guidance on watch-finishing
materials, which no installed skill covers.

**Honest note on what no skill would have helped with.** The Fourier tooth count
and the equidistant-stones observation were the two things that actually held up,
and neither is in any skill's scope. The self-criticism above should be accurate,
not maximal.

## Things that will bite the next agent

All of these are in `HANDOVER.md` in more detail. The short list:

- Run `om10_check.py` after `om10_extract.py`, every time, or the tooth counts
  revert to broken ones. `cad_parts.py` now refuses to proceed if they are wrong.
- `build123d` is patched inside `.venv-cad` to survive a malformed Windows font.
  Reinstalling it breaks every CAD script at once.
- Render, then `dotnet build`, then screenshot. `Assets/` is copied into `bin/`
  at build time, so a screenshot taken before the rebuild shows the old assets.
  This cost me a wrong conclusion about the dial once already.
- `clash_check.py` deliberately no longer checks the moving parts.
  `tools/interfere_check.py` does, by boolean intersection, and is not wired into
  `render.ps1` because it costs minutes and only changes when placement does.
- The OM10 STEP is not in the repo. `tools/om10_extract.py` reads it from
  `C:\Users\jason\Downloads\`.
