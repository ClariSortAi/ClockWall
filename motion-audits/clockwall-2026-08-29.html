ClockWall — the states are polished, the transitions don't exist

An always-on 1080×1920 portrait wall display: analogue clock, machine meters, and the Claude Code sessions running on the machine. Read from across a room, touched almost never.

Overall

This codebase has unusually good motion discipline for a v1: exactly two animations exist, both looped attention signals with written rationale, tuned to read from across a room. What's missing is everything in between — every discrete state change on the wall is a hard cut. Rows appear, vanish, and change order with no continuity; the centerpiece analogue face moves with less personality than a microwave clock. Under the chosen delight weighting, the work is not restraint — it's adding the connective tissue, then spending one or two flourishes where a wall clock has earned them.

What's off

The distribution is inverted. The only nonzero durations on the wall belong to two infinite loops, while every one-time transition sits at zero. A healthy profile is the mirror image: state changes living in the 150–350ms responsive band, loops rare and earned. The two dots on the right are fine — they are signals, not transitions, and their slowness is the point. The six hollow dots on the left are the audit.

CriticalAll lenses

Two infinite loops ignore the OS animation-effects setting

WhatThe busy-dot breath and the needs-you flash run forever, and nothing in the codebase consults the user's reduced-motion choice. Windows exposes it as UISettings.AnimationsEnabled (Settings → Accessibility → Visual effects → Animation effects); a panel that is lit all day in someone's field of vision is precisely where that setting must be honored.

Why it mattersLooping motion that cannot be paused is the checklist's hardest accessibility line. And the app already has a perfect non-motion fallback: the accent border, the sort-to-top, and the "N NEEDS YOU" header line convey attention with zero movement, so honoring the setting costs nothing in function.

Recommended motionGate storyboard.Begin() in ApplyPulse on new UISettings().AnimationsEnabled. Checked per call, it self-heals through the same periodic poll the rest of the app already trusts — house style. When false, leave the glow and dot at full, steady opacity: signal without motion, as the demo shows.

The roster's signature state change has no chosen motion

WhatWhen a session flips to waiting it is Moved to the top of the list, and when priorities shift, rows change order — but AgentListView declares no ItemContainerTransitions, so whether rows glide or teleport is left to platform defaults nobody chose.

Why it mattersThis is the one state change the wall exists to broadcast. A viewer across the room who sees a different row on top has to re-scan the list; a viewer who sees the row travel keeps its identity for free. Spatial continuity is not decoration here — it is the information.

Recommended motionDeclare the intent: a TransitionCollection on the ListView with RepositionThemeTransition (staggering off) so displaced rows slide to their new slots on the system curve (~300ms). Verify on the wall; if defaults were already animating this, the declaration still turns an accident into a decision.

Session rows materialize and vanish with no enter or exit

WhatA new session's card pops in fully formed; an ended session's card is simply gone on the next poll. Nothing marks arrival or departure.

Why it mattersOn an ambient display, an instant appearance is indistinguishable from "was that always there?" A brief materialize tells the room something started; a subtler, faster exit says something finished without demanding a look. His rule: exits always quieter than enters.

Recommended motionAddDeleteThemeTransition in the same TransitionCollection as the reposition fix — one XAML block buys both findings. If its stock feel reads heavy on the wall, a custom enter of opacity 0→1 with an 8px rise (~250ms) is the XAML translation of his web recipe; the blur layer of that recipe doesn't translate cheaply to XAML and should be skipped, not faked.

The analogue ⇄ digital swap hard-cuts between two hero layouts

WhatApplyMode flips two Visibility flags: the 640px face and the 400px digits trade places between one frame and the next.

Why it mattersThis is the largest visual change the wall can make, on its most-looked-at zone, at monthly frequency — the exact case where the frequency gate welcomes a considered transition. Emil's keyboard rule says never animate a key press, and that dissent is recorded; his rule guards flows people run hundreds of times a day, not a ceremony performed twice a season on a display whose whole job is being looked at.

Recommended motionA ~250ms crossfade: outgoing element fades to 0 at scale 0.985, incoming fades in from scale 0.97 — opacity and scale only, both composition-friendly. Flip Visibility when the fade lands. Gate it on the same AnimationsEnabled check as the loops; the instant swap is the correct reduced-motion form and already exists.

Issues to address
ImportantJhey

The second hand has the least personality of anything on the wall

WhatSecondAngle.Angle = now.Second * 6 — once a second, the hand teleports 6°. No sweep, no tick, no settle. The centerpiece of a display that exists to be looked at moves like a property assignment.

Why it mattersEasing has personality, and a clock is the one UI element with a century of motion vocabulary to borrow from. A quartz tick with a tiny overshoot settle reads as mechanical from across the room — alive, precise, and worth the wall it hangs on. This is the flagship of the chosen delight weighting.

Recommended motionEach tick, animate Angle from the previous position to the new one over ~140ms with a slight overshoot ease (BackEase, small amplitude, or keyframes). It's a dependent animation on the UI thread, but 140ms once per second is negligible — and when AnimationsEnabled is false, the current instant snap is already the correct fallback. The continuous 60s sweep is the alternative personality; the tick suits a wall that already speaks in discrete seconds.

Lens reference summary

Lens referenced most: Jakub Krehel — Production Polish. The chosen weighting asked for delight, but nearly every finding turned out to be connective tissue: the app's states are finished and its transitions are absent, which is polish work before it is play. Jhey supplies the two flourishes that make it a showpiece; Emil's restraint is the reason this audit is short, and his keyboard-rule dissent on the face swap is recorded rather than overruled quietly.

To follow Emil more strictly: skip the face-swap crossfade, keep every discrete change instant, and ship only the reduced-motion gate and the list transitions.
To follow Jakub more strictly: add the two opportunity cross-fades (context registers, ticker) so no text on the wall ever changes without a 150–200ms acknowledgment.
To follow Jhey more strictly: prototype the continuous sweep second hand next to the quartz tick and pick by feel; then take the hourly swell further — minute-hand shimmer at noon, a midnight moment.
